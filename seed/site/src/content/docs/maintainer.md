I'm **Oleg Botvenko**, a team lead at [Automattic](https://automattic.com/).

I built exobrain in February 2026 to launch a new product with my team there — our first AI-native project. With agents doing the execution, the bottleneck became knowledge stuck in people's heads, so we ran the whole launch through a shared exobrain instead:

- **Nobody knew everything, and nobody had to.** Every finding and decision went through an agent into the exobrain, which held the product's current state. To find out where things stood, you asked your agent, not a colleague.
- **The team coordinated agent-to-agent.** People worked in parallel, and their agents read, wrote and reconciled the same knowledge. There were no status meetings; the shared space carried the coordination.
- **Every write was reviewed like a deploy.** Whatever landed in the exobrain became true for everyone, so an agent proposed each change and a person approved it.

The idea behind that way of working is on the [philosophy](/philosophy/) page.

In June 2026 I lifted the patterns that held up into this public seed. The same machinery runs my household: health checks, document filing, reminders, and a family agent used by people who don't write code.

Exobrain is a personal project, not an Automattic one. One person maintains it in the open, with no support commitment; the [changelog](/changelog/) is the record of what changes.
